\title{WISE: Zero-Cost Connectivity on Wrench-Feasible Assignment\\
Fibers for Heterogeneous Multi-Robot Transport}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Jorge Luis Mayorga Taborda}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Universidad Internacional de Valencia (VIU)}\\
Valencia, Spain\\
jl.mayorga236@gmail.com}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Heterogeneous multi-robot transport couples physical task allocation with the
communication structure needed to coordinate it. We show that when productive utility
depends on a low-dimensional served aggregate, its unique optimum can correspond to a
\emph{positive-dimensional fiber} of wrench-feasible robot compositions---an unexploited
resource for communication design.
We characterize the fiber on its active face and give a tangent-cone condition for
improving algebraic connectivity without moving the optimal served aggregate, including
the case of a repeated Fiedler eigenvalue. A wrench- and information-self-sustaining
equilibrium (WISE) is then selected lexicographically by a semidefinite program, and the
resulting spectral capacities separate \emph{zero-cost}, \emph{costly}, and
\emph{impossible} connectivity requirements through a price of self-sustainability
$\Price(\sigma)$ whose subgradient prices connectivity in units of utility. For finite
teams we give a sufficient, conservative margin test under which an integer recovery
preserves both wrench and connectivity feasibility. Simulations with heterogeneous teams
exhibit a zero-cost connectivity plateau (in a representative instance $\lamtwo$ spans
$\approx0$ to $0.78$ at fixed productive value); an exact integer oracle bounds the
integrality gap for $N\le8$, while structured rounding attains re-certified integer
self-sustaining assignments on all $30$ instances at $N=12$ (no optimality claim). The
guarantees concern the affine communication-backbone relaxation;
distributed online realization over a changing geometric network remains future work.
\end{abstract}
