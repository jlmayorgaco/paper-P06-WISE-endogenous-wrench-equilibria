\title{WISE: Zero-Cost Connectivity within Wrench-Feasible Assignment\\
Fibers for Heterogeneous Robot Transport}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Jorge Luis Mayorga Taborda}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Universidad Internacional de Valencia (VIU)}\\
Valencia, Spain\\
jl.mayorga236@gmail.com}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Heterogeneous multi-robot transport couples physical task allocation with the communication
structure needed to coordinate it. We show that when productive utility depends on a
low-dimensional served aggregate, its unique optimal \emph{served aggregate} can correspond to
a \emph{positive-dimensional fiber} of wrench-feasible robot compositions, a resource for
communication design. We derive a free-networkability modulus $\Gamma_{\Emanifold}$ that is
positive \emph{iff} a strictly better-connected composition exists anywhere on the fiber, so
$\Gamma_{\Emanifold}=0$ certifies global connectivity optimality, repeated Fiedler eigenvalues
included. A wrench- and information-self-sustaining equilibrium (WISE) is selected
lexicographically by a semidefinite program, and the spectral capacities separate
\emph{zero-cost} (zero loss of productive utility), \emph{costly} and \emph{impossible}
requirements. The resulting margin is an \emph{operational budget}: attenuating every relay
conductance by $\rho$ preserves the certificate up to an explicit $\rho_{\mathrm{cert}}$,
equal to $1-\sigmastar/\lamtwo$ when the base graph is disconnected, which WISE maximizes over
the fiber. The affine formulation is validated on $N{=}12$ instances, where the directional
certificate vanishes at every SDP selector tested. A \emph{separate} $N{=}6$ pair-dependent
integer stress test, solved by exhaustive enumeration, illustrates the composition exchange and
the fixed-assignment attenuation guarantee: a hard-threshold selector and pair-WISE preserve
the same productive value and finish the nominal mission indistinguishably, yet certify
$10.8\%$ against $24.8\%$ relay attenuation. On the tested $N\le8$ instances exact enumeration
puts the attainable integer self-sustaining gap below $0.06\%$ of $V^{\star}$, reported
separately from the loss of a rounding heuristic.
\end{abstract}
