\title{WISE: Zero-Cost Connectivity on Wrench-Feasible Fibers\\
for Heterogeneous Robot Transport}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Jorge Luis Mayorga Taborda}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Universidad Internacional de Valencia (VIU)}\\
Valencia, Spain\\
jl.mayorga236@gmail.com}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Heterogeneous multi-robot transport couples physical task allocation with the
communication structure needed to coordinate it. We show that when productive utility
depends on a low-dimensional served aggregate, its unique optimum can correspond to a
\emph{positive-dimensional fiber} of wrench-feasible robot compositions---a resource for
communication design. We characterize the fiber on its active face and give a tangent-cone
condition for improving algebraic connectivity without moving the optimal served aggregate,
including a repeated Fiedler eigenvalue. A wrench- and information-self-sustaining equilibrium
(WISE) is then selected lexicographically by a semidefinite program, and the resulting
spectral capacities separate \emph{zero-cost}, \emph{costly}, and \emph{impossible}
connectivity requirements through a price of self-sustainability $\Price(\sigma)$ whose
subgradient prices connectivity in units of utility. For finite teams a conservative margin
test preserves joint wrench and connectivity feasibility after integer recovery. Simulations
exhibit a zero-cost plateau ($\lamtwo$ rises $\approx0\!\to\!0.41$ at fixed productive value);
an exact oracle measures the integrality gap for $N\le8$, and structured rounding attains
re-certified integer self-sustaining assignments on all $30$ instances at $N=12$ (no
optimality claim). Zero-cost is exact on the relaxed optimal fiber; finite-team integerization
may incur a separately quantified recovery loss. The guarantees concern the affine
communication-backbone relaxation; distributed online realization remains future work.
\end{abstract}
