\title{WISE: Zero-Cost Connectivity within Wrench-Feasible Assignment\\
Fibers for Heterogeneous Robot Transport}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Jorge Luis Mayorga Taborda}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Universidad Internacional de Valencia (VIU)}\\
Valencia, Spain\\
jl.mayorga236@gmail.com}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Heterogeneous multi-robot transport couples productive task allocation with the communication
graph required to coordinate it. When utility depends on a low-dimensional served aggregate,
the aggregate optimum is unique while its wrench-feasible robot compositions can form a
\emph{positive-dimensional fiber}. We define a free-networkability modulus $\Gamma_{\Emanifold}$
that is positive iff a better-connected point exists on that fiber and vanishes iff the current
point is globally connectivity-optimal, repeated Fiedler eigenvalues included. For an affine role-induced Laplacian, a wrench- and
information-self-sustaining equilibrium (WISE) selects that point through a lifted semidefinite
program while preserving the productive optimum exactly, and the resulting margin is an
\emph{operational budget}: attenuating every relay conductance by $\rho$ preserves the
certificate up to an explicit $\rho_{\mathrm{cert}}$. The affine method is validated on
$N{=}12$ instances by directional and optimality checks and an independent primal, wrench,
aggregate and spectral re-evaluation. A separate exact
$N{=}6$ integer stress test, with pair-dependent link gains outside the affine model, shows
what the reserve is worth: a hard-threshold selector and pair-WISE preserve the same productive
value $V^{\star}$ and finish the nominal mission indistinguishably, yet certify $10.8\%$
against $24.8\%$ relay attenuation. Exact small-team enumeration and
structured recovery quantify integer attainability separately.
\end{abstract}
