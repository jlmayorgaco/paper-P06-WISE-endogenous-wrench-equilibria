\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Cooperative transport by a heterogeneous robot team couples two decisions usually studied
apart: which robots apply force to a load, and which relay the information that lets the team
agree on that force. Coordinating such teams from locally communicated
aggregates~\cite{martinezpiazuelo2022} presumes the graph needed to \emph{compute} the
assignment is available while it is being computed; for a mobile team it is not, as the robots
that compute it are the robots whose placement forms the graph. An
assignment can be productively optimal, delivering the demanded wrench, yet commit every robot
to lifting and leave none to relay, so connectivity collapses and the estimator loses its
channel.

Population-game and distributed Nash-seeking formulations take the interaction graph as given.
Topology-constrained multi-robot allocation~\cite{williams2017matroid} and spectral graph
design~\cite{ghosh2006growing} address related components; heterogeneous MRTA adds physical
coalition constraints~\cite{calvo2025heterogeneous}, and backbone methods reconfigure the relay
layer~\cite{santos2024backbone}. Each imposes connectivity as a \emph{constraint}, scalarizes
it against utility, or repairs the backbone after the fact. To the best of our targeted review
we are not aware of a formulation that characterizes the geometry of the \emph{exact}
wrench-feasible productive-optimum set and uses its neutral directions to decide whether
connectivity is free. Throughout, \emph{zero-cost} means zero loss of the productive utility
$V$, not zero energy, time, communication or role change. We exploit a structural
degeneracy: the payoff couples only through aggregates, so the productive optimum fixes the
served aggregate but leaves an aggregate-invariant fiber of wrench-feasible compositions
(Thm.~\ref{thm:degeneracy}), over which connectivity is selected lexicographically.

\emph{Relation to task-priority control.} Exploiting redundancy for a free secondary objective
is an established idea: null-space task-priority control lets lower-priority behaviors use
kinematic redundancy without perturbing a primary task~\cite{antonelli2010nsb}. Here the
redundancy is not a kinematic Jacobian but a \emph{wrench-constrained assignment-optimum
polytope}, the secondary objective is the \emph{nonsmooth} algebraic connectivity, and
$\Gamma_{\Emanifold}=0$ is equivalent to \emph{global} connectivity optimality over the whole
fiber, repeated Fiedler eigenvalues included. The analysis builds on established results:
concavity of $\lamtwo$ in affine weights~\cite{fiedler1973,ghosh2006growing}, derivatives of a
repeated minimum eigenvalue~\cite{lewis1996spectral}, and LMI connectivity design. Novelty is
claimed only for that fiber, the local--global certificate on it, and its attainability.
\emph{Scope:} equilibrium-set geometry and planner-side selection, not distributed Nash
seeking, actuator-level realization or hardware.

\emph{Contributions.} \textbf{(i) Optimal-fiber networkability}
(Thms.~\ref{thm:degeneracy},~\ref{thm:networkability}): the optimal aggregate is unique but the
equal-payoff assignments form a fiber whose dimension is the local loss of strong monotonicity,
whose positive dimension is \emph{equivalent} to loss of strong monotonicity on the active
composition face, and one scalar $\Gamma_{\Emanifold}$ is positive iff a strictly
better-connected optimum exists \emph{anywhere} on it, zero iff the point is globally
connectivity-optimal.
\textbf{(ii) Selection, price and operational attenuation bound}
(Thm.~\ref{thm:existence}, Cors.~\ref{cor:tv} and~\ref{cor:budget}): WISE is a lexicographic
refinement computed by SDP, $\LamE\le\LamX$ separate \emph{free}, \emph{costly} and
\emph{impossible} requirements, clearing $\sigmadyn$ stabilizes a reduced information-layer
interface at a rate bound that survives a moving graph, and the reserve is a certified
relay-degradation budget WISE maximizes. \textbf{(iii) Assessment}: an exact small-team oracle,
re-certified recovery, and a planar closed-loop transport simulation.
