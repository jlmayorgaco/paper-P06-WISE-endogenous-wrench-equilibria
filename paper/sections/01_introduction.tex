\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Cooperative transport by a heterogeneous robot team couples two decisions usually
studied apart: which robots apply force to a load, and which relay the information
that lets the team agree on that force. Distributed equilibrium-seeking and
population-game methods coordinate such teams by having each agent revise its
strategy from locally communicated aggregates, converging to an equilibrium of a
constrained potential game~\cite{sandholm2010population,quijano2017role,%
barreiro2016constrained,martinezpiazuelo2022}. These methods rest on one premise:
the communication graph needed to \emph{compute} the equilibrium is available while
the equilibrium is being reached.

For a mobile team this cannot be taken for granted: the robots that compute the
equilibrium are the robots whose placement forms the graph. An assignment can be
productively optimal---delivering the demanded wrench (force and
torque~\cite{murray1994manipulation})---yet commit every robot to lifting, leaving none to
relay, so algebraic connectivity collapses and the estimator loses the consensus channel
it needs.

Population-game and distributed Nash-seeking formulations take the interaction graph
as given~\cite{quijano2017role,barreiro2016constrained,koshal2016aggregative}.
Communication-aware task allocation does couple the two, jointly trading task utility
against the connectivity induced by the chosen
assignment~\cite{williams2017matroid,lin2021connectivity,zavlanos2011connectivity,%
ghosh2006growing,prorok2017diversity}. We instead exploit a structural degeneracy: because
the payoff couples only through aggregates~\cite{jensen2010aggregative}, the productive
optimum fixes the served aggregate but leaves an aggregate-invariant fiber of
wrench-feasible compositions (Theorem~\ref{thm:degeneracy}). We select connectivity
lexicographically over that fiber, without moving the productive aggregate, which yields a
relaxed SDP existence certificate under hard wrench constraints.

\paragraph*{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Equilibrium-fiber geometry} (Theorem~\ref{thm:degeneracy},
Prop.~\ref{prop:freeconn}): the optimal served aggregate is unique, but the equal-payoff
assignments form a fiber whose dimension is the loss of strong monotonicity; a projection
criterion certifies when connectivity improves at \emph{zero} productive cost.
\item \textbf{WISE certificate and selection} (Theorem~\ref{thm:existence}): the fiber
contains a composition meeting the connectivity requirement $\sigmastar$ iff a semidefinite
program clears it, and a lexicographic selector returns the most-connected productive
optimum without moving the aggregate.
\item \textbf{Information-layer relevance and validation} (Proposition~\ref{thm:stability}):
a small-gain condition $c\,\mF\,\lamtwo>\vt_1\vt_2$ ties the requirement to
optimizer--estimator stability, and simulations quantify the margin, integer recovery, and
the heuristic attainment gap.
\end{itemize}
A productively optimal, wrench-feasible assignment whose induced graph meets the estimator's
stability requirement is a \emph{WISE equilibrium}.
