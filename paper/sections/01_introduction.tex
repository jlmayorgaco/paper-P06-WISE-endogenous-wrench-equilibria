\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Cooperative transport by a heterogeneous robot team couples two decisions usually
studied apart: which robots apply force to a load, and which relay the information
that lets the team agree on that force. Distributed equilibrium-seeking and
population-game methods coordinate such teams by having each agent revise its
strategy from locally communicated aggregates, converging to an equilibrium of a
constrained potential game~\cite{sandholm2010population,quijano2017role,%
barreiro2016constrained,martinezpiazuelo2022}. These methods rest on one premise:
the communication graph needed to \emph{compute} the equilibrium is available while
the equilibrium is being reached.

For a mobile team this premise cannot be taken for granted, because the robots that
compute the equilibrium are the robots whose placement forms the graph. Consider a
team moving a load across a gap. An assignment can be productively
optimal---delivering the demanded wrench (force and
torque~\cite{murray1994manipulation})---yet commit every robot to lifting, leaving
none to relay: algebraic connectivity collapses and the estimator that produced the
assignment loses the consensus channel it needs to compute it.

Population-game and distributed Nash-seeking formulations take the interaction graph
as given~\cite{quijano2017role,barreiro2016constrained,koshal2016aggregative}.
Communication-aware task allocation does couple the two, jointly trading task utility
against the connectivity induced by the chosen
assignment~\cite{williams2017matroid,lin2021connectivity,zavlanos2011connectivity,%
ghosh2006growing,prorok2017diversity}. Our distinction is sharper: because the payoff
couples only through aggregates~\cite{jensen2010aggregative}, the productive optimum
fixes the served aggregate but leaves an \emph{aggregate-invariant} fiber of
wrench-feasible compositions (Theorem~\ref{thm:degeneracy}); we select connectivity
\emph{lexicographically} over that fiber---without moving the productive
aggregate---which yields an SDP existence certificate under hard wrench constraints.

\paragraph*{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Equilibrium-fiber geometry} (Theorem~\ref{thm:degeneracy}): the
productively optimal served aggregate is unique, but the equal-payoff assignments
form a fiber whose dimension equals the tangent nullspace of its minimal active
face; the transportation value $(T\!-\!1)(K\!-\!1)$ is one special case.
\item \textbf{WISE existence and selection} (Theorem~\ref{thm:existence}): the fiber
contains a composition meeting the robust connectivity requirement $\sigmastar$ iff
the optimal value of a semidefinite program clears it, and a lexicographic selector
then returns the most-connected productively optimal assignment without moving the
aggregate.
\item \textbf{Dynamic relevance and validation} (Theorem~\ref{thm:stability}): the
spectral condition $c\,\mF\,\lamtwo>\vt^2$ makes the coupled
served-aggregate/estimator subsystem exponentially stable---sufficient in general,
necessary in the isotropic modal case---and simulations with heterogeneous
second-order unicycles evaluate the certificate, the predicted boundary, and a
decentralized realization.
\end{itemize}
We call a productively optimal, wrench-feasible assignment whose induced communication
substrate meets the estimator's stability requirement a \emph{WISE equilibrium}.
