\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Cooperative transport by a heterogeneous robot team couples two decisions usually studied
apart: which robots apply force to a load, and which relay the information that lets the team
agree on that force. Distributed equilibrium-seeking and population-game methods coordinate
such teams by having each agent revise its strategy from locally communicated aggregates,
converging to an equilibrium of a constrained potential game~\cite{martinezpiazuelo2022}. All
rest on one premise: the communication graph needed to \emph{compute} the equilibrium is
available while it is being reached. For a mobile team that cannot be assumed---the robots that
compute the equilibrium are the robots whose placement forms the graph. An assignment can be
productively optimal, delivering the demanded wrench, yet commit every robot to lifting and
leave none to relay, so connectivity collapses and the estimator loses its channel.

Population-game and distributed Nash-seeking formulations take the interaction graph as given.
Communication-aware task allocation couples the two, trading utility against assignment-induced
connectivity~\cite{williams2017matroid,ghosh2006growing}; heterogeneous MRTA solves integer
coalition allocation under physical constraints~\cite{calvo2025heterogeneous}, and backbone
methods reconfigure the relay layer~\cite{santos2024backbone}. Each imposes connectivity as a
\emph{constraint}, scalarises it against utility, or repairs the backbone after the fact; to
the best of our targeted review, none characterizes the geometry of the \emph{exact}
wrench-feasible productive-optimum set and uses its neutral directions to decide whether
connectivity is attainable at zero productive loss. We exploit a structural degeneracy: since
the payoff couples only through aggregates, the productive optimum fixes the served aggregate
but leaves an aggregate-invariant fiber of wrench-feasible compositions
(Theorem~\ref{thm:degeneracy}), over which connectivity is selected lexicographically.

\emph{What is and is not new.} The mechanics are standard---concavity of $\lamtwo$ in affine
weights~\cite{fiedler1973,ghosh2006growing}, derivatives of a repeated minimum
eigenvalue~\cite{lewis1996spectral}, LMI connectivity design. The contribution is the object
they are applied to: the wrench-feasible productive-optimum set is a positive-dimensional
polytope whose tangent cone carries the entire zero-loss question, condensed into one scalar.
\emph{Scope:} equilibrium-set geometry and centralized selection, not distributed Nash seeking.

\emph{Contributions.} \textbf{(i) Optimal-fiber networkability}
(Thms.~\ref{thm:degeneracy},~\ref{thm:networkability}): the optimal served aggregate is unique
but the equal-payoff assignments form a fiber whose dimension is the local loss of strong
monotonicity, and one scalar $\Gamma_{\Emanifold}$ is positive iff a strictly better-connected
optimum exists \emph{anywhere} on it, zero iff the point is globally connectivity-optimal
(repeated Fiedler eigenvalues included). \textbf{(ii) Selection, price and what it buys}
(Thm.~\ref{thm:existence}, Cor.~\ref{thm:price}, Prop.~\ref{thm:stability},
Cor.~\ref{cor:tv}): WISE is a lexicographic refinement of the productive
variational-equilibrium set computed by SDP; $\LamE\le\LamX$ separate \emph{free},
\emph{costly} and \emph{impossible} requirements; and clearing the derived threshold
$\sigmadyn$ gives exponential stability of a reduced information layer, with the same certified
lower rate bound under a moving physical graph. \textbf{(iii) Finite-team and closed-loop
assessment}: an exact small-team oracle, directly re-certified recovery, and a planar
closed-loop transport simulation with rigidly attached robots in which productively identical
assignments succeed and fail. Distributed realization remains future work.
