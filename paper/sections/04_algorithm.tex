\section{Centralized Relaxed Certificate}
\label{sec:algorithm}

A WISE is decided by the SDP of Theorem~\ref{thm:existence}, solved centrally over the fluid
relaxation: Stage~1 computes a productive optimum
$z^{\mathrm{prod}}\in\arg\max_{z\in X_{\mathrm f}}V(z)$ and sets the unique optimal aggregate
$y^{\star}=Bz^{\mathrm{prod}}$; the Stage-2 SDP~\eqref{eq:sdp} then returns $\LamE$ and the
most-connected optimal assignment $z^{\star}_{\mathrm{WISE}}$, with $\LamE\ge\sigmastar$ the
relaxed existence test. We use this
certificate as the relaxed reference; an integer team is recovered by rounding and
\emph{directly} re-certified against budgets, wrench feasibility and the spectral requirement,
Proposition~\ref{prop:integer} serving as a separate conservative radius.

\emph{Structured recovery.} Given a relaxed $z^{\star}$, one draw of
\emph{occupancy-aware sequential randomized rounding} processes robots in a uniformly random
order: robot $i$ samples from $z^{\star}_i$ restricted to the currently available
unit-capacity actions (free contact slots and relay sites), with idle always admissible and
assigned when the remaining mass is zero, renormalizing after each capacity update. Every draw
thus meets one action per robot and all capacities exactly (randomized rounding, \emph{not} a
marginal-preserving scheme). A greedy \emph{repair} then raises connectivity over the single-robot candidate moves
$\mathcal D(\hat z)=\{e_{ir}{-}e_{ia}:\hat z_{ia}{=}1,\ r\ \text{a free relay}\}$: a move is
admissible only if $\hat z{+}d\in\mathcal X$ and the lifted wrench LP~\eqref{eq:tensor} stays
feasible; the admissible move maximizing $\lamtwo(\Lap(\hat z{+}d))$ is applied and accepted
only if $\Delta\lamtwo>\tau_\lambda$. Since integer assignments are finite and $\lamtwo$
strictly increases, the search cannot cycle; it stops when re-certified, when no admissible
move remains, or after $K_{\max}{=}12$ moves (each iteration costs $|\mathcal D|$ wrench-LP and
eigenvalue evaluations); it returns $\hat z$ once budgets, wrench, and $\lamtwo\ge\sigmastar$
hold. Empirically ($N{=}12$, $30$ seeds, Section~\ref{sec:exp}) one draw is already self-sustaining
on $18/30$ instances and the remaining $12$ are repaired in a single relay move (max $1$, cap
$12$). Repair trades productive value for connectivity ($Bd\neq0$; median loss
$2.3\%\!\to\!4.9\%$), so the team is integer self-sustaining but rarely \emph{exactly}
zero-cost ($4/30$, $\|B\hat z-y^{\star}\|_\infty\le\tau_B$).

A fully \emph{distributed} realization---each robot sampling from a local flow and converging
to $\Emanifold^{\star}_{\mathrm{WISE}}$ over the graph it forms---needs a convergence guarantee
over a decision-dependent graph and is left to future work; we claim only the centralized
certificate.
