\section{Centralized Relaxed Certificate}
\label{sec:algorithm}

Existence and selection of a WISE are decided by the semidefinite program of
Theorem~\ref{thm:existence}, solved centrally over the fluid relaxation. Stage~1 fixes
$y^{\star}$ by maximising $V$ over $X_{\mathrm f}$; the Stage-2 SDP~\eqref{eq:sdp} then
returns $\LamE$ and the most-connected productively optimal assignment
$z^{\star}_{\mathrm{WISE}}$, and $\LamE\ge\sigmastar$ is the relaxed WISE existence test
(Theorem~\ref{thm:existence}). We report this certificate and use it as the relaxed
reference for the simulations; an integer team is recovered by rounding and \emph{directly}
re-certified against budgets, wrench feasibility and the spectral requirement, with
Proposition~\ref{prop:integer} reported separately as a conservative robustness radius.

\emph{Structured recovery.} Given $z^{\star}$: \textbf{(i)}~\emph{dependent rounding} draws
one action per robot, preserving each unit budget and slot/relay occupancy
($\sum_i z_{i,a}\le1$; taken slots are resampled out); \textbf{(ii)}~solve the lifted wrench
LP for the integer team; \textbf{(iii)}~while $\lamtwo<\sigmastar$, apply the single-robot
move $\hat z\!+\!d$ ($Ad=0$, occupancy-preserving) that keeps wrench feasibility and most
increases $\lamtwo$ (a marginal lifter/idler $\to$ relay); \textbf{(iv)}~stop when
re-certified or after $\le12$ moves. Step~(iii) trades productive value for connectivity
($Bd\neq0$), so the recovered team is integer self-sustaining but rarely \emph{exactly}
zero-cost.

A fully \emph{distributed} realization---each robot sampling from a local flow and converging
to $\Emanifold^{\star}_{\mathrm{WISE}}$ over the graph it forms---needs a message protocol and
a convergence guarantee over a decision-dependent graph, and is left to future work; we claim
only the centralized certificate here.
