\section{Simulation Study}
\label{sec:exp}

We validate the main results on heterogeneous teams ($N=12$ unless noted) in a two-region
world: a rigid load demands a planar wrench, short links connect robots only within a
cluster, and second-order unicycles apply a heading-aligned elliptical force, so the gap
is bridged only when a long-range type relays. The primary metric is the \emph{certified
service rate}: served only when wrench-feasible \emph{and} $\lamtwo-\sigmastar\ge0$. Every
instance is seeded, reproduces exactly, and is backed by a numerical certificate.

\paragraph*{E1 --- The fiber is real and certified (Fig.~\ref{fig:central}a,b)}
At the productive optimum the neutral space $\mathcal N_p=\ker A\cap\ker B\cap\ker G_I$ has
dimension $59$; moving along it holds $V$ and $Bz$ at machine precision (residuals
$<10^{-8}$) while $\lamtwo$ crosses $\sigmastar$---Theorem~\ref{thm:degeneracy} made
numerical, with the safe subset strictly inside the optimal fiber.

\paragraph*{E2 --- Same fiber, opposite fates (Fig.~\ref{fig:central}c,d)}
Because the relay action carries no served capacity, flipping one spare long-range robot
between \emph{idle} and \emph{relay} moves along the optimal fiber exactly: both integer
compositions share $Bz=y^{\star}$, $V^{\star}$, and zero wrench residual, yet
$\lamtwo(\Lgeo)$ jumps from $0.03$ (fragmented) to $0.88$ (bridged) across
$\sigmastar=0.25$. Poses come from the actual pipeline, not drawn by hand; a closed-loop
transport (repository, \texttt{exp\_physical}) confirms Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge} along a
trajectory.

\paragraph*{E3 --- Lexicographic selection beats scalarization}
On free-regime seeds ($\LamE\ge\sigmastar$, classified per seed by the exact SDP), the
scalarised $V_\varepsilon=V+\varepsilon\lamtwo$ pays for connectivity: as $\varepsilon$
grows from $10^{-3}$ to $1$ its productive loss $V^{\star}-V$ rises from $0$ to $0.049$
\emph{and} its aggregate drift $\|Bz-y^{\star}\|$ from $0$ to $0.307$, both staying below
the Tikhonov envelopes $\varepsilon\,\mathrm{osc}(\lamtwo)$ and
$\sqrt{2\varepsilon\,\mathrm{osc}(\lamtwo)/\alpha}$ (repository, \texttt{exp\_epsilon}),
whereas the lexicographic WISE selector holds both at zero---the exact-optimum
preservation a weighted sum cannot match.

\paragraph*{E4 --- Integer recovery (Rem.~\ref{rem:integer})}
The fluid certificate carries over to integer robots: over $16$ seeds the fluid optimum is
certified $100\%$ of the time, randomized rounding recovers a certified integer
assignment on $100\%$ (argmax rounding $75\%$), with a mean welfare integrality gap of
$-0.009$. Robots are integer throughout; no fractional robot is implied.

\paragraph*{E5 --- Method comparison (Table~\ref{tab:ablation})}
At a demanding operating point ($\nu=0.4$, $\tau_d=6.5$), single-requirement and random
$\mathcal X$-feasible assignments almost never clear both the wrench and connectivity
tests. The \emph{exact} WISE SDP certifies every seed, whereas the decentralized flow
(\texttt{wise\_pd}, $50\%$) and a centralized multistart heuristic ($67\%$) fall
short---the honest cost of not solving the SDP. The exact SDP phase boundary
$\LamE=\sigmastar$ over $(\nu,\tau_d)$ has perfect recall against the flow (repository,
\texttt{exp\_phase}).
