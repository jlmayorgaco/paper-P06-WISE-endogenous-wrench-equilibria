\section{Simulation Study}
\label{sec:exp}

We validate the main results on heterogeneous teams ($N=12$ unless noted) in a two-region
world: a rigid load demands a planar wrench, short links connect robots only within a
cluster, and the gap is bridged only when a long-range type relays. The heterogeneity is
\emph{conflicting}: the long-range type has both higher lifting capacity ($c_\ell>c_s$) and
stronger relay gain ($\gamma_\ell>\gamma_s$), so it is the more valuable resource for both
roles and WISE must reorganize a whole composition to free it for the relay it alone bridges.
Integer assignments are embedded in a geometric simulator solely to evaluate the induced
communication graph $\Lgeo(q)$; closed-loop transport dynamics are outside the present
validation. The primary metric is the \emph{certified service rate}: served only when
wrench-feasible \emph{and} $\lamtwo-\sigmastar\ge0$. Every instance is seeded and reproduces
exactly.

\paragraph*{E1 --- Fiber neutrality, certified (Fig.~\ref{fig:central}a)}
At an interior optimum $\mathcal N_p=\ker A\cap\ker B$ has dimension $72-13=59$; moving along
it holds $V,Bz$ at machine precision ($<10^{-8}$) while $\lamtwo$ crosses $\sigmastar$, so the
connectivity-feasible subset lies strictly inside the fiber.

\paragraph*{E2 --- Comparative-advantage exchange (Fig.~\ref{fig:central}b,c)}
Within the heterogeneous team a matched long/short pair (capacities equalized to isolate one
neutral direction) swaps roles at fixed $Bz=y^{\star}$, $V^{\star}$ and zero wrench residual:
the productively optimal composition (long lifts, short relays) leaves the clusters unbridged
($\lamtwo(\Lgeo)\approx0$), whereas WISE (short lifts, long relays) bridges them
($\lamtwo(\Lgeo)=0.78$); only connectivity differs.

\paragraph*{E3 --- Lexicographic vs.\ scalarized selection}
On free-regime seeds $V+\varepsilon\lamtwo$ trades productive value for connectivity (loss
$0\!\to\!0.049$, drift $0\!\to\!0.307$ as $\varepsilon:10^{-3}\!\to\!1$), whereas the
lexicographic selector holds both at zero---a formulational, not empirical, gap.

\paragraph*{E4 --- Integer recovery and an exact oracle (Rem.~\ref{rem:integer}, Table~\ref{tab:oracle})}
At $N=12$ a single randomized-rounding draw re-certifies a fluid optimum with probability
$0.40$ and best-of-$30$ re-certifies $29/30$ seeds (argmax $7/30$); the \emph{rounding loss}
$(V^{\star}_{\mathrm{relax}}-V_{\mathrm{round}})/V^{\star}_{\mathrm{relax}}=1.8\%$ (mean). For
$N\le8$ we enumerate \emph{every} pure assignment exactly (Table~\ref{tab:oracle}): whenever a
relaxed WISE exists an integer WISE also exists ($0\%$ false positives) and the true
integrality gap $V^\star-V^{\mathbb Z}$ is $\le0.002$---the relaxation is tight and
integer-recoverable on these instances.

\paragraph*{E5 --- Selection among productive optima (Table~\ref{tab:ablation})}
Single-criterion baselines rarely clear both tests. The hard connectivity-constrained
optimum ($\max V$ s.t.\ $\lamtwo\ge\sigmastar$) also reaches $V^{\star}$, but in the free
regime it may return \emph{any} point of the optimal face and merely \emph{satisfies} the
requirement (margin $+0.15$). WISE is not a competitor to it but its lexicographic
\emph{tie-break}: among the same connectivity-constrained productive optima it selects the
maximum-margin point ($+2.55$). The relaxed SDP solves in $30$--$60$\,ms at $N=12$.

\paragraph*{E6 --- Price of self-sustainability (Thm.~\ref{thm:price}, Fig.~\ref{fig:price})}
Sweeping $\sigma$ traces $\Price(\sigma)$ over $40$ seeds: zero up to $\LamE$ (free), positive
on $(\LamE,\LamX]$ (costly), infeasible beyond (impossible), matching \eqref{eq:trichotomy} on
every seed ($\LamE<\LamX$, median costly width $0.64$). The subgradient
$\pi^{\star}\in\partial\Price$ prices one unit of connectivity.
