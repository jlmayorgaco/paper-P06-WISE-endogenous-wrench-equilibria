\section{Simulation Study}
\label{sec:exp}

We validate the main results on heterogeneous teams ($N=12$ unless noted) in a
two-region world: a rigid load in the right cluster demands a planar wrench, and short
links connect robots only within a cluster, so the gap is bridged only when a
long-range type relays. The physical layer integrates a rigid load and second-order
unicycles whose lifting force is confined to the heading-aligned elliptical set. The
primary metric is the \emph{certified service rate}: a load counts as served only when
it is wrench-feasible \emph{and} $\lamtwo-\sigmastar\ge0$. Every instance is seeded and
reproduces exactly.


\paragraph*{E1 --- Empirical attainment and self-defeat (Thm.~\ref{thm:stability})}
The decentralized heuristic~\eqref{eq:pd} drives $\lamtwo(\Lap(z))$ above
$\sigmastar$ and the wrench residual to zero together, the price $\pi$ settling on
long-range relays. Comparing two fiber compositions traces the stability boundary:
the estimation error $\|e(t)\|$ decays when a long-range robot relays and grows when
it is withdrawn ($\lamtwo<\sigmastar$), with the sign change at
$c\,\mF\,\lamtwo=\vt^2$.

\paragraph*{E2 --- Closed-loop transport (Fig.~\ref{fig:physical}, Lem.~\ref{lem:bridge})}
Four lifters---each force confined to its heading-aligned elliptical set---carry a
rigid load toward the gap while one long-range robot relays. The load tracks its
reference pose with a residual the nonholonomic team almost fully delivers, and the
geometric connectivity $\lamtwo(\Lgeo(q))$ tracks the surrogate $\lamtwo(\Lap)$ with
the Weyl bound $\lamtwo(\Lap)-\varepsilon_L$ above $\sigmadyn$ throughout: the
guarantee of Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge} holds along the whole trajectory, not just at the
plan.

\paragraph*{E3 --- Regime phase diagram (Fig.~\ref{fig:phase}, Cor.~\ref{thm:trichotomy})}
Sweeping the long-range fraction $\nu$ and torque demand $\tau_d$, we solve the WISE
SDP~\eqref{eq:sdp} in every cell and overlay its exact boundary $\LamE=\sigmastar$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:phase}). The two predicted failure edges appear: existence fails as
$\nu\!\to\!0$ (no long-range robot can relay) and wrench feasibility fails at high
$\tau_d$. Against the decentralized flow the SDP verdict has \emph{perfect recall}
over $144$ runs (no false negatives; $61$ TP, $64$ TN, $19$ FP, $0$ FN): its only
misses are cells the exact certificate clears but the heuristic flow does not reach.

\paragraph*{E4 --- Method comparison (Table~\ref{tab:ablation})}
At a demanding operating point ($\nu=0.4$, $\tau_d=6.5$), enforcing either requirement
alone fails: \texttt{scalar\_capacity} and \texttt{wrench\_only} fragment the network,
\texttt{connectivity\_only} cannot lift, and a random $\mathcal X$-feasible assignment
(budget/occupancy only) almost never clears the wrench and connectivity tests. The
\emph{exact} WISE SDP certifies every seed, whereas the decentralized flow
(\texttt{wise\_pd}, $50\%$) and even a centralized multistart heuristic ($67\%$) fall
short. The gap between the exact certificate and the heuristic solvers is the honest
cost of not solving the SDP---evidence for the certificate, not a claim of distributed
optimality.

\paragraph*{E5 --- Emergent roles (Ex.~\ref{ex:fiber})}
Pooled over seeds, relay commitment falls on long-range types by comparative advantage
(Ex.~\ref{ex:fiber}): a neutral long/short exchange leaves $Bz$ fixed while raising
$\lamtwo$---the predicted division of labour, not a general theorem.
