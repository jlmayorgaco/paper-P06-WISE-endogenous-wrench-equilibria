\section{Simulation Study}
\label{sec:exp}

We validate on heterogeneous teams ($N=12$ unless noted) in a two-region world bridged only
when a long-range type relays. The heterogeneity is \emph{conflicting}---that type has both
higher capacity and relay gain---so WISE must reorganize a whole composition to free it. The
primary metric is the \emph{certified service rate} (served only when wrench-feasible
\emph{and} $\lamtwo\ge\sigmastar$). All experiments use fixed recorded seeds, and every
reported number is written to a manifest by the script producing it.

\paragraph*{E1 --- Non-trivial composition exchange (Fig.~\ref{fig:flagship})}
The two-load flagship ($N=12$, $c_L=2c_S$) certifies a comparative-advantage
reorganization: releasing one long lifter for two short ones and moving it to the relay holds
$Bz=(3,3)=y^{\star}$, both wrenches and $V=V^{\star}$ (aggregate preserved to $<10^{-8}$),
while $\lamtwo(\Lgeo)$ rises $\approx0\!\to\!0.41$ (Thm.~\ref{thm:networkability},
$\Gamma_{\Emanifold}>0$); here $\dim\Emanifold=58$. A three-region instance similarly frees \emph{two} long robots.
On $200$ symmetric $\lamtwo{=}\lambda_3$ instances the eigenspace criterion matches the
one-sided finite difference to median error $10^{-6}$ (sign correct $200/200$) versus $0.04$
for a single eigenvector; Loewner dominance held in $10{,}000/10{,}000$ perturbations---a
construction check of Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge}, not an independent test of
Assumption~\ref{as:standing}(iv).

\paragraph*{E1b --- The networkability certificate is global ($12$ seeds)}
The local--global equivalence~\eqref{eq:iff} holds on $12/12$ seeds: the certificate
vanishes at the selector ($\Gamma_{\Emanifold}\le3.4\cdot10^{-6}$ versus $[0.45,1.78]$ at an
interior fiber point), the bound~\eqref{eq:gammabound} holds throughout, and the closed form
agrees with the general SDP to $2\cdot10^{-12}$. Degeneracy and \emph{network-visible}
degeneracy differ by an order of magnitude (Rem.~\ref{rem:dnet}): $\dim\Emanifold=59$ while
$d_{\mathrm{net}}\in[10,12]$.

\paragraph*{E5 --- Information-layer stability at zero productive cost (Fig.~\ref{fig:modal})}
With $\lamtwo$ from the flagship record and the scenario's own gains
($\sigmadyn=0.25$), the two productively identical assignments straddle the threshold: the
productive-only optimum has $\lamtwo=4.4\cdot10^{-16}$, so $\det A_{\lamtwo}=-0.25$ and
$\alpha=-0.207$---the information layer \emph{diverges}, growing $461\times$---while WISE has
$\lamtwo=0.415>\sigmadyn$, $\alpha=+0.128$ and $\|e(T)\|/\|e(0)\|=2.1\cdot10^{-2}$; the
analytic rate~\eqref{eq:rate} matches the observed slope to $5.6\cdot10^{-17}$. This is the
reduced information layer only, but it is what $\sigmastar$ exists to secure, at $\Delta V=0$.

\paragraph*{E2 --- Integer recovery and an exact oracle}
At $N=12$, structured recovery over $30$ instances $\times\,30$ draws ($900$ recoveries) is
jointly feasible in $653/900$ ($72.6\%$) before repair and $895/900$ after (mean $0.27$, max
$1$ move). Draws are nested within instances, so the \emph{instance} is the unit: the mean
per-instance rate is $99.4\%$ (cluster bootstrap $[98.3,100.0]$, $20{,}000$ resamples; median
$100\%$, min $83.3\%$), with $30/30$ instances succeeding on $\ge1$ draw---a pooled Wilson
$[98.7,99.8]$ would wrongly treat draws as independent. Recovery loss is median $4.2\%$ (max $27\%$) and $83/895$ ($9.3\%$)
recoveries are exactly zero-cost---a property of this heuristic, not of the attainable integer
optimum. A loss-budgeted repair recovers $514/900$ at $\eta_{\max}{=}5\%$, $806/900$ at
$10\%$. The worst-case radius is far from tight (ratio to the achieved
rounding distance: median $191$), certifying $0/30$ seeds where direct re-certification
accepts $29/30$. For $N\le8$ an exact oracle enumerates \emph{every} $|\mathcal A|^N$ map
($8$ seeds per $N$): no false positives at the design requirement ($0/23$) and
$\max g_{\mathbb Z}^{\mathrm{ss}}\le0.005$ ($<0.06\%$ of $V^{\star}$); pushed
\emph{adversarially} to $\sigma=0.97\LamE$ it is not always integer-tight at $N{=}6$
($13/50$), none at $N{=}7,8$.

\paragraph*{E7/E4 --- When is connectivity free, and what does it cost?}
On a $6\times6\times4$ grid, wrench demand is \emph{inert}
($\LamE$ constant at $1.572$ across all six $\tau_d$; no facet binds), while the bridging
type governs everything: as $\nu$ grows $0.17\to0.67$, $\LamE$ rises $0.82\to2.48$ and the
costly band widens $0.10\to0.97$---scarcity compresses \emph{both} the free regime and the
merely-expensive one. Sweeping $\sigma$ over $40$ further seeds traces $\Price$ as predicted
(median costly width $0.64$), the dual matching the finite difference over $84$ points
($r=0.996$). Two controls close the loop: the hard connectivity-constrained optimum also
reaches $V^{\star}$ but returns \emph{any} point of that face ($+0.15$ versus WISE's $+2.55$),
and a scalarised $V+\varepsilon\lamtwo$ moves the aggregate at \emph{every} tested
$\varepsilon\in[10^{-3},1]$ (drift $2\cdot10^{-4}$ to $0.284$) while its productive loss stays
near zero below $\varepsilon\approx0.1$: at a maximiser $V$ is flat to second order but the
aggregate moves to first. WISE attains zero drift and zero loss with no weight to calibrate.
