\section{Simulation Study}
\label{sec:exp}

We validate the main results on heterogeneous teams ($N=12$ unless noted) in a two-region
world: a rigid load demands a planar wrench, short links connect robots only within a
cluster, and second-order unicycles apply a heading-aligned elliptical force, so the gap
is bridged only when a long-range type relays. The primary metric is the \emph{certified
service rate}: served only when wrench-feasible \emph{and} $\lamtwo-\sigmastar\ge0$. Every
instance is seeded, reproduces exactly, and is backed by a numerical certificate.

\paragraph*{E1 --- Fiber neutrality, certified (Fig.~\ref{fig:central}a,b)}
At an interior productive optimum the neutral space $\mathcal N_p=\ker A\cap\ker B\cap
\ker G_I$ has dimension $72-\operatorname{rank}[A;B]=59$ (no inequality active there);
moving along it holds $V$ and $Bz$ at machine precision (residuals $<10^{-8}$) while
$\lamtwo$ crosses $\sigmastar$, so the connectivity-feasible subset lies strictly inside
the optimal fiber.

\paragraph*{E2 --- Same fiber, different connectivity (Fig.~\ref{fig:central}c,d)}
Because the relay action carries no served capacity, flipping one long-range robot between
\emph{idle} and \emph{relay} moves along the optimal fiber exactly: both integer
compositions share $Bz=y^{\star}$, $V^{\star}$, and zero wrench residual, yet
$\lamtwo(\Lgeo)$ rises from $\approx0$ (disconnected) to $0.85$ (connected with margin)
across $\sigmastar=0.25$. Poses come from the actual pipeline, not placed by hand; a
closed-loop transport (repository, \texttt{exp\_physical}) confirms Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge}
along a trajectory.

\paragraph*{E3 --- Lexicographic vs.\ scalarized selection}
On free-regime seeds ($\LamE\ge\sigmastar$, classified per seed by the exact SDP), the
scalarised $V_\varepsilon=V+\varepsilon\lamtwo$ trades productive value for connectivity:
as $\varepsilon$ grows from $10^{-3}$ to $1$ its productive loss $V^{\star}-V$ rises from
$0$ to $0.049$ \emph{and} its aggregate drift $\|Bz-y^{\star}\|$ from $0$ to $0.307$, both
under the Tikhonov envelopes $\varepsilon\,\mathrm{osc}(\lamtwo)$ and
$\sqrt{2\varepsilon\,\mathrm{osc}(\lamtwo)/\alpha}$ (repository, \texttt{exp\_epsilon}),
whereas the lexicographic WISE selector holds both at zero.

\paragraph*{E4 --- Integer recovery (Rem.~\ref{rem:integer})}
Over $30$ seeds the fluid optimum is WISE-certified $100\%$ of the time; a single
randomized-rounding draw certifies $66\%$, best-of-$30$ draws $100\%$, and one-hot argmax
$67\%$. The relative welfare gap $V_{\mathrm{relax}}-V_{\mathrm{int}}$ is $+1.8\%$ on
average ($+4.5\%$ worst). Every robot is integer; the SDP certifies the relaxation and
integer candidates are independently re-certified.

\paragraph*{E5 --- Method comparison (Table~\ref{tab:ablation})}
At a demanding operating point ($\nu=0.4$, $\tau_d=6.5$), single-requirement and random
$\mathcal X$-feasible assignments almost never clear both the wrench and connectivity
tests. The \emph{exact} WISE SDP certifies every seed, whereas the decentralized flow
(\texttt{WISE-PD}, $50\%$) and a centralized multistart heuristic ($67\%$) fall short---the
observed attainment gap of not solving the SDP. Over the $(\nu,\tau_d)$ sweep every
empirically attained solution lay inside the SDP-feasible region (flow success $\Rightarrow$
WISE existence); certified-but-unattained cells quantify the heuristic attainment gap
(repository, \texttt{exp\_phase}).
