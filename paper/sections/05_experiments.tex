\section{Simulation Study}
\label{sec:exp}

We validate the main results on heterogeneous teams ($N=12$ unless noted) in a
two-region world: a rigid load in the right cluster demands a planar wrench, short links
connect robots only within a cluster, and second-order unicycles apply a heading-aligned
elliptical lifting force, so the gap is bridged only when a long-range type relays. The
primary metric is the \emph{certified service rate}: a load counts as served only when
it is wrench-feasible \emph{and} $\lamtwo-\sigmastar\ge0$. Every instance is seeded and
reproduces exactly. Consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:stability}, the decentralized
heuristic~\eqref{eq:pd} drives $\lamtwo$ above $\sigmastar$ and the wrench residual to
zero together, and the estimation error decays only once a long-range robot relays, with
the sign change at $c\,\mF\,\lamtwo=\vt^2$.

\paragraph*{E1 --- Spatial clustering and the network effect (Fig.~\ref{fig:cluster})}
Robots group into two clusters separated by a gap wider than the short-range radius, so
the geometric graph has two components ($\lamtwo\approx0$) unless a long-range robot
occupies the gap (Fig.~\ref{fig:cluster}a--b). Sweeping the relay position
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cluster}c), $\lamtwo(\Lgeo)$ clears $\sigmastar$ only near the gap
centre: the composition controls exactly this network effect. A closed-loop rigid-load
transport (repository, \texttt{exp\_physical}) confirms Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge} along a
trajectory, $\lamtwo(\Lgeo(q))$ staying within $\varepsilon_L$ of $\lamtwo(\Lap)$
throughout.

\paragraph*{E2 --- Fiber mechanism vs.\ scalarization}
Moving along a productive-nullspace direction holds $V\equiv V^{\star}$ and $Bz=y^{\star}$
at machine precision while $\lamtwo$ crosses $\sigmastar$: the degeneracy is real and the
safe subset lies strictly inside the optimal fiber (\texttt{exp\_fiber}). Against the
scalarised $V_\varepsilon=V+\varepsilon\lamtwo$ over $8$ seeds, as $\varepsilon$ grows
from $10^{-3}$ to $1$ the productive loss $V^{\star}-V$ rises from $0$ to $0.049$
\emph{and} the aggregate drift $\|Bz-y^{\star}\|$ from $0$ to $0.307$, whereas WISE holds
both at zero---the exact-optimum preservation a weighted sum cannot match.

\paragraph*{E3 --- Regime phase diagram (Fig.~\ref{fig:phase}, Cor.~\ref{thm:trichotomy})}
Sweeping the long-range fraction $\nu$ and torque demand $\tau_d$, we solve the WISE
SDP~\eqref{eq:sdp} in every cell and overlay its exact boundary $\LamE=\sigmastar$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:phase}). Both predicted failure edges appear: existence fails as
$\nu\!\to\!0$ and wrench feasibility fails at high $\tau_d$. Against the decentralized
flow the SDP verdict has \emph{perfect recall} over $144$ runs ($61$ TP, $64$ TN, $19$
FP, $0$ FN); its only misses are cells the certificate clears but the heuristic does not
reach.

\paragraph*{E4 --- Method comparison (Table~\ref{tab:ablation})}
At a demanding operating point ($\nu=0.4$, $\tau_d=6.5$), enforcing either requirement
alone fails: \texttt{scalar\_capacity} and \texttt{wrench\_only} fragment the network,
\texttt{connectivity\_only} cannot lift, and a random $\mathcal X$-feasible assignment
(budget/occupancy only) almost never clears the wrench and connectivity tests. The
\emph{exact} WISE SDP certifies every seed, whereas the decentralized flow
(\texttt{wise\_pd}, $50\%$) and even a centralized multistart heuristic ($67\%$) fall
short---the honest cost of not solving the SDP, evidence for the certificate rather than
a claim of distributed optimality.

\paragraph*{E5 --- Emergent roles (Ex.~\ref{ex:fiber})}
Pooled over seeds, relay commitment falls on long-range types by comparative advantage:
a neutral long/short exchange leaves $Bz$ fixed while raising $\lamtwo$---the predicted
division of labour, not a general theorem.
